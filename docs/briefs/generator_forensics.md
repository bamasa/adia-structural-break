Work carefully and empirically; report only what the data supports.

## Data
- `structural-break-real-time-test/data/X_train.parquet`: MultiIndex (id, time), columns `value` (float), `period` (1 = history/pre-boundary, 2 = online). 10 000 series, history length 1000–5000, online length 10–999.
- `structural-break-real-time-test/data/y_train_index.parquet`: index id, columns `tau_index` (position of the break inside the online part; -1 if no break) and `tau` (absolute time). About 50% of series have a break. Labels are per (id, time) in `y_train.parquet` (target 1 from tau on).
- Metric context: at each online step t, a cross-sectional AUC over all series alive at t, label = (t >= tau); ~83% of metric weight on steps 100–700.

## What we already know (do not redo, build on it)
- A break-type anatomy on 300-point windows either side of tau found: variance-only 130 series, dependence-only (AR(1) change) 125, variance+shape 72, shape-only 48, and 353 of 728 breaks (~40%) with no visible change in mean, variance, lag-1 autocorrelation or kurtosis. Nineteen further second-to-fourth-order statistics (ACF to lag 30, volatility clustering, spectral mass, entropy, tail quantiles, zero crossings, nonlinearity term, rolling-mean variance) did not separate those 353 from matched controls (non-breaking series cut at a random point). Windows of 600 gave the same picture.
- History length and series id carry no label information.

## Tasks (in this order; write intermediate results to files as you go)
1. Marginal structure of histories (standardised per series): distribution shape (kurtosis, skew), discreteness (rounding, integer values, repeated values), zero-inflation, bounded ranges, asymmetric tails. Cluster series into a few marginal families and give counts.
2. Dynamic structure of histories: ACF/PACF to lag 20, ACF of squares (ARCH), unit-root/trend (variance ratio, simple ADF-like), seasonality (dominant spectral peaks and their periods), regime switching (e.g. bimodality of rolling variance). Propose a small set of generating families (e.g. white noise, AR(p), MA, ARMA, GARCH, random-walk-like, seasonal, regime-switching, mixtures) and assign each series to the best-fitting family with a simple criterion; give counts. Check whether families are clean (as if generated from a menu) or continuous.
3. Break characterisation using ALL available post-break points and the full history as pre-break: per broken series estimate the change in mean, sd, AR coefficients (orders 1–3 via least squares), kurtosis, skew, ACF of squares, tail index (Hill-type on |x|), spectral slope, and the RATIO of innovation variance under the history-fitted AR model before/after. Report magnitude distributions per family. Crucially: do the changes look drawn from a discrete menu (e.g. sd multiplied by fixed factors) or from continuous distributions? Are there minimum magnitudes?
4. The 353 "invisible" breaks: using the full post-break segment (not 300 points) and a matched-control design (non-breaking series cut at the same relative position), test a broader set of statistics than before: Hurst exponent / long-memory (rescaled range and DFA), permutation entropy, sample entropy, spectral slope, count of jumps (|diff| > 4 robust sd), runs test, time-reversibility (third-order moment of differences), BDS-style nonlinearity via correlation-integral proxy, ACF of |x| at lags 1–50, quantile crossing rates, distance between empirical CDFs (KS, Wasserstein) after standardisation, and a change in the AR-fit residual distribution (kurtosis of residuals). For each statistic report the ratio of break-spread to control-spread and the fraction beyond 2 control sigmas. Also check whether "invisible" breaks have shorter post-break segments than the visible ones (i.e. are they invisible only because tau is late). Conclude plainly whether any signature exists.
5. Synthetic generator draft: write `synth.py` that generates series by family with break types and magnitudes drawn from the distributions you estimated; generate 500 series and compare 10–15 summary statistics against 500 real series (histograms/quantiles side by side in a text table). Report where it matches and where it does not.
6. Deliver a report `REPORT.md` in the forensics folder (English, concise, tables, with the scripts' names), and return a summary in your final message: the generating families with counts, the break menu with magnitudes, the verdict on the 353 invisible breaks, how faithful the synthetic draft is, and the 3 most promising modelling consequences for improving cross-sectional AUC at steps 100–700.

Do not fabricate numbers: everything in the report must come from computations you actually ran. If something cannot be determined, say so.
